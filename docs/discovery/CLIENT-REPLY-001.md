## Why option 2, and a date you need

I would have chosen it anyway, but there is something I should have told you earlier.

**My quarterly branch review is on 20 December.** That is the meeting where I look at the eleven branches and decide what happens to each of them for the next quarter — including the branch I nearly closed last time.

Four weeks from now puts your core delivery around 5 December. That gives me a fortnight of real use before the meeting, which means I walk into it with numbers I have actually tested rather than numbers I am seeing for the first time.

If you had delivered everything at six weeks, it would have landed on 19 December. One day before. I would have had no time to trust it, and I would have gone into that meeting with the spreadsheet again.

So option 2 is not a compromise for me. **It is the only one of the three that is useful.**

This is my fault for not telling you what the system is for in calendar terms. You asked thirteen questions about my data; I should have volunteered the one date that matters.

## The phone, which you were right to expect me to argue about

You predicted correctly.

But I think you have made it bigger than it is, so let me narrow it.

**I do not need the whole system on my phone.** I am not going to resolve product matches on a train. What I do, when I am travelling, is open something and look at how the branches are doing.

So: **one screen, read-only, that works on a small screen.** Sales by branch for a date range, with the fairness and completeness markers you described. Nothing else. No uploading, no review queue, no settings.

If that is a day rather than a week, I would like it in the first four. If it is still a week, leave it where you have it and I will live until mid-December.

Tell me which it is.

## Three things before you talk to Rania

### 1. She is worried about her job

Nobody has said this out loud, so I will.

Rania has spent most of every Sunday on this for three years. It is visible work, and she knows I can see it. When I told her a system was coming to do it, she said the right things, but I have known her a long time.

**If you walk in and ask what the worst part of her Sunday is, you may get a careful answer.**

I would say something like this at the start, and you can quote me: *the point is to stop her spending Sundays fixing exports so that she can do the work I actually need her for, which is the part that requires someone who knows the business.* That is true, and it is also my plan.

You will get a much better interview if she believes it.

### 2. She knows things I do not

She has been looking at these files every week for three years. She will know which branch always breaks, which product names are a mess, which manager exports at lunchtime, and what she has quietly been fixing by hand without mentioning it.

**That last category is the valuable one.** There will be corrections she makes every week that she thinks are too small to be worth describing. Those are exactly the rules your system needs, and they are not written anywhere.

### 3. Ask her to show you, not tell you

If you can, do it on a Sunday, or get her to open last Sunday's files and work through them in front of you.

What people say they do and what they do are different things, and in her case the difference is the specification.
