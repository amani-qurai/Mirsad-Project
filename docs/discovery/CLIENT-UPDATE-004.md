## Jericho is not one of them

You listed it as a variation, and I would have too. But **08:00–19:00 is eleven hours, exactly the same as 09:00–20:00.** Jericho is *shifted earlier*, not *shorter*.

It still matters — a sale at 08:30 lands in a part of the day no other branch is trading, so hour-by-hour comparison needs care — but Jericho belongs in the normal group, and marking it as handicapped would be inventing a problem.

One correct figure is worth more than a long list.

## Salfit is the one I would look at next

You read it as *"finding its feet slowly."* It is open **nine hours against everyone else's eleven** — the largest handicap in the chain, in the branch where you have the most money at risk and the least history to judge by.

Some of the gap is the hours. I cannot tell you how much until there is a quarter of clean data to measure.

**But I am not going to "adjust" it away, and I want to say why now rather than when you see it on screen.**

The obvious move is to divide everything by hours and show sales-per-hour, so all eleven look comparable. **I am not doing that**, because Salfit genuinely does sell less in absolute terms. It is open fewer hours by choice. And the absolute figure is the one that pays its rent.

So you will always get **both, side by side**: the raw total, the sales per trading hour, and the deficit stated in words. Normalising would hide one true thing in order to show another — which is the same failure as hiding the handicap, pointed the other way.

**Adjusting is not the fix for a hidden handicap. Declaring is.**
